# Workout routine presets

Research and product guidance for the first-launch routine picker in liftmaxxing.

## Recommendation

Ask **“How do you usually train?”** and offer five choices:

| Preset | Typical schedule | Default editable chips |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Full body | 2–3 days per week | `Full body` |
| Upper / Lower | 4 days per week | `Upper`, `Lower` |
| Push / Pull / Legs | 3–6 days per week | `Push`, `Pull`, `Legs` |
| Muscle groups | Usually 5 days per week | `Chest`, `Back`, `Shoulders`, `Legs`, `Arms` |
| Custom | Any schedule | No defaults; user adds their own |

These cover the common structures named by the National Strength and Conditioning Association: full body, upper/lower, push/pull/legs, and muscle-group splits. The Custom option covers hybrids without making onboarding noisy.

## Product behavior

1. Show the five presets as large selectable cards on first launch.
2. After selection, preview the seeded workout chips.
3. Let the user rename, add, and delete chips before continuing.
4. Treat the preset as a one-time starting point. Save only the resulting editable workout types; do not keep them coupled to the preset.
5. Keep routine editing available from the main screen or settings.

Do not ask for goals, experience, equipment, or an exact weekly calendar yet. None of those are required to start tracking PRs.

## Research notes

- Full-body training two or three times weekly is commonly recommended as a simple starting structure. Split routines become useful when someone wants four or more weekly sessions and more per-session focus. [American Council on Exercise](https://www.acefitness.org/continuing-education/certified/may-2024/8630/mastering-the-art-of-the-split-routine/)
- NSCA teaching material explicitly lists four common weekly structures: three-day full body, four-day upper/lower, five-day muscle groups, and six-day push/pull/legs. [NSCA Coach, issue 10.1](https://www.nsca.com/contentassets/81db35c5b91a424586f0133024b1da62/nsca-coach-10.1.5-approaching-group-fitness-using-a-classroom-framework.pdf)
- Upper/lower splits alternate regions and make it straightforward to train each region twice weekly while allowing recovery. [NASM upper/lower guide](https://www.nasm.org/resource-center/blog/training/upper-lower-splits-explained)
- A 2024 systematic review of 14 studies found no meaningful strength or muscle-growth difference between split and full-body routines when training volume was equal. The app should therefore support user preference instead of labeling one routine as “best.” [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38595233/)
- ACSM’s 2026 guidance emphasizes choosing a routine that fits the person’s schedule, comfort, and goals because consistency drives long-term results. [ACSM resistance-training summary](https://www.acsm.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Resistance-Training-Position-Stand-infographic.pdf)

## Scope note

The sources establish that these are commonly used programming structures; they do not provide reliable consumer market-share rankings. The onboarding order above prioritizes simplicity and breadth, not claimed popularity percentages.
